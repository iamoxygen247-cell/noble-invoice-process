1 · What is changing and why

Three changes to the Content Understanding analyzer and the decision engine:

ChangeBehavior
New field account_number Required (critical) for municipal bills — a municipal bill missing it routes to human review. Optional for commercial invoices — captured and written when present, never blocks. Uses the extract + generate twin method proven on vendor_name and po_or_job_number.
po_or_job_number prompts The PO number sometimes appears inside parentheses ( ) or square brackets [ ]. Both twin descriptions gain a sentence to strip the brackets. Pure addition — nothing else in those prompts changes.
invoice_number Required (critical) for municipal bills; optional for commercial invoices — same policy as account_number. Also: the value can appear near a License Number / License No. / License # label (business-licence renewals), and the field is converted from a single extract field into a full extract + generate twin.

Confirmed decisions

  • Account-number label scope (broad): Account/Acct #, Customer Account, Utility Account, Customer #, Client #, Folio, Roll Number all count. Licence/Permit numbers are excluded — they belong to invoice_number now.
  • Normalization: strip internal spaces, keep dashes/dots as printed ("1234 5678 9012" → "123456789012"; "12345-001" stays). Twin agreement compares letters+digits only, case-insensitive. No strict format regex (unlike the PO 110/330 rule).
  • Commercial bills: both fields captured when present, purely informational.
  • Dynamics: account_number is added to writeValues; the Power Automate flow maps it separately (outside this repo).
  • Forced edit: the current invoice_number description says “Do not return … license number …”, which contradicts the new licence-label rule — that entry comes out of the negative list.
Why this is a small engine change: the decision engine is data-driven. gates.evaluate resolves every entry in TWIN_FIELDS, build_write_values writes every twin final, and only fields listed in the bucket’s critical_fields() can block. So the entire required-for-municipal / optional-for-commercial behavior is: two TWIN_FIELDS entries + a new MUNICIPAL_DELTA = ("account_number", "invoice_number"). Zero per-field engine code.

2a · New account_number twins

account_number_extract — method: extract (verbatim printed value)
Customer account number assigned by the biller — the stable identifier for the customer's ongoing account with the vendor, utility, or municipality, exactly as printed on the document. May be labeled Account Number, Account No., Account #, Acct #, Acct No., Acct Number, Customer Account, Customer Account Number, Utility Account, Utility Account Number, Customer Number, Customer #, Customer No., Client Number, Client #, Client No., Folio, Folio Number, Folio No., or Roll Number. It usually appears in the account summary block near the top of the bill and again on the remittance stub or payment return slip at the bottom. Remove any spaces printed between digit or letter groups, but keep dashes, dots, and all other punctuation exactly as printed (for example '1234 5678 9012' becomes '123456789012'; '12345-001' stays '12345-001'). Letters are allowed. Do not return the invoice number, statement number, bill number, PO number, job number, work order number, GST registration number, business number, meter number, rate class, licence number, permit number, cheque number, phone or fax number, a date, or a dollar amount. Return null if no account number is printed.
account_number_generate — method: generate (step-by-step reasoning validator)
Determine the customer's account number on this bill — the stable identifier the biller (vendor, utility, or municipality) has assigned to the customer's account. Reason step by step over the entire document: 1. The account number identifies the customer's ongoing account with the biller, so it is unchanged from bill to bill. It is usually printed in the account summary near the top of the bill and repeated on the remittance stub or payment return slip at the bottom — an identifier printed in both places is almost always the account number. 2. Scan the whole document — account summary, header, customer/service block, payment instructions, and remittance stub — and collect every candidate labeled Account Number, Account No., Account #, Acct #, Acct No., Customer Account, Customer Account Number, Utility Account, Utility Account Number, Customer Number, Customer #, Customer No., Client Number, Client #, Client No., Folio, Folio Number, Folio No., or Roll Number. 3. Do not confuse it with per-bill identifiers: an invoice number, statement number, or bill number changes with every bill and is never the account number. If a candidate is labeled Invoice, Statement, or Bill, discard it and keep looking. 4. Never answer with a PO number, job number, work order number, GST registration number, business number, meter number, rate class, licence number, permit number, cheque number, phone or fax number, a date, a dollar amount, or any part of an address or postal code. 5. If multiple distinct candidates remain, prefer the one printed on the remittance stub; on a property-tax bill the Folio or Roll Number is the account number. 6. Normalize the answer: remove spaces printed between digit or letter groups, but keep dashes, dots, and all other punctuation exactly as printed (for example '1234 5678 9012' means 123456789012; '12345-001' stays 12345-001). Before answering, verify the value is the customer's account identifier and not an invoice or statement number. Return an empty string only if, after scanning the entire document, no account number appears anywhere.

2c · invoice_number — twin conversion + licence labels

The existing field is renamed invoice_number_extract. Its description keeps the existing text with exactly two edits:

  • Label list extended: “…Inv No., Tax Invoice No., Bill Number, Document Number, License Number, License No., License #, Licence Number, or Licence No.” (both US and Canadian spellings — BC municipal documents print “Licence”).
  • “license number” removed from the “Do not return …” negative list (would contradict the line above). Everything else stays verbatim.
invoice_number_generate — method: generate (new twin)
Determine the unique invoice identifier the vendor assigned to this invoice. Reason step by step over the entire document: 1. Prefer the value in the invoice header or top-right invoice table directly labeled Invoice #, Invoice No., Invoice Number, Inv #, Inv No., Tax Invoice No., Bill Number, or Document Number. It is often printed near the invoice date. 2. On a business licence or permit renewal bill, the identifier may instead be labeled License Number, License No., License #, Licence Number, or Licence No. — on such bills that value is the invoice number. 3. If the document includes both an invoice page and a work order page, prefer the value labeled Invoice # on the invoice page; use a standalone '#:' value on the work order page only if it matches the invoice page number. 4. Never answer with a P.O. No., PO number, job number, work order number, project number, reference number, customer number, account number, vendor GST number, tax ID, phone number, postal code, product registration number, a date, a subtotal, GST amount, total amount, balance due, or a service address. 5. Return only the identifier value, not the label. Before answering, verify the value identifies THIS invoice and is not the customer's account number or a PO/job number. Return an empty string only if no invoice identifier appears anywhere on the document.

def _identifier_values_agree(a: Any, b: Any) -> bool:
    """True when two identifiers carry the same non-empty letter+digit sequence,
    case-insensitively. Punctuation/spacing is display formatting and is ignored
    for agreement; the written value keeps the extract's printed punctuation."""
    na = re.sub(r"[^0-9a-z]", "", str(a).lower()) if a is not None else ""
    nb = re.sub(r"[^0-9a-z]", "", str(b).lower()) if b is not None else ""
    return na != "" and na == nb

TWIN_FIELDS = {
    ...
    INVOICE_FINAL: (INVOICE_EXTRACT, INVOICE_GENERATE, _identifier_values_agree, False),
    ACCOUNT_FINAL: (ACCOUNT_EXTRACT, ACCOUNT_GENERATE, _identifier_values_agree, False),
}

How the twins resolve (existing resolve_twin contract — unchanged)

  • extract passes on its own when confidence ≥ 0.73 — its printed value is written.
  • agreement both twins below threshold but the letter+digit sequences match — passes; the literal extract value is written.
  • generate extract found nothing and the generate twin is confident — “generate rescue” fills it in.
  • review a present extract that disagrees with the generate twin is never overridden; on a municipal bill a failing resolution routes to human review (B4). On a commercial bill it is advisory only.

7 · Risks and notes

  • Municipal bills gain two blocking fields. A municipal bill lacking an account-like identifier OR any invoice/statement/licence number routes to review — expected per requirement. Watch utility statements on the first harness runs. Commercial invoices are unaffected.
  • Licence/permit numbers are deliberately excluded from account_number — they belong to invoice_number.
  • writeValues.account_number is a new key — harmless until the Power Automate flow maps it, but the value won’t land in Dynamics until then (external to this repo).
  • The analyzer rename changes the raw decision-JSON shape (fields.invoice_number becomes the resolved final plus two raw twins), but writeValues.invoice_number keeps its key — no Power Automate change needed for it.
  • Prompt-quality risk: a generate twin could latch onto an adjacent identifier on unusual layouts; on municipal bills a disagreement routes to review (safe), on commercial it is advisory only. Prompts can be tuned after sample runs.
